Minimal update. Paths are relative to the `policyhub` folder of your repo.

WHAT THIS ADDS

  A SECOND INSURED on an opportunity, for survivorship policies.

  Tick "Two insureds" on the deal and a second block opens: name, date of
  birth, sex, state, and that person's OWN life expectancy with its own
  provider and report date.

  The return is modelled on WHICHEVER ESTIMATE RUNS OUT LATER, compared
  as dates and never as month counts. Each estimate is counted from its
  own report, so a 72-month report written this year outlasts an 84-month
  one written two years ago; comparing months would pick the wrong life
  every time the reports are dated apart, which is most of the time.

  This is the later of two medians and deliberately NOT a joint life
  expectancy — a floor on the wait rather than the expectation of it. The
  screen and both one-pagers say so, and the "24 months late" row is
  where it gets tested.

  On the Sommers file: Gerald 36 months, Judith 71, both written 26
  August. Hers governs. Priced off his alone the deal reads 83.9%; off
  the correct life, 31.1%.

  DOCUMENT READING now decides whether two LE reports are two opinions on
  one person or one opinion each on two people, by comparing the names on
  the certificates. Two different people fills the second insured and
  ticks the box; two reports on the same person stay a second opinion.
  When the names cannot be told apart it assumes two opinions, which is
  the safer way to be wrong.

  NOTE: the reading itself happens in the valuation service, not here.
  This build maps its answer. If that service does not yet pull the name,
  date of birth, sex, report date and month count off a 21st Services
  certificate, that is a change there and not in this repo.

FOUR DEFECTS FIXED ALONG THE WAY

  1. schema.sql ran an ALTER above the CREATE TABLE it depended on. Fine
     on a database that already had the table; the application REFUSED TO
     START on an empty one. Any new environment would have hit it.

  2. The analysis carried assembled full names to investors, in a payload
     where every other name is scrubbed to initials.

  3. The de-identifier did not know the new insured2_ columns, so a second
     insured's name reached investors unmasked. It now gets its own
     initial rather than sharing the first insured's.

  4. A privacy check read "if the investor holds any policies…" against a
     fixture where none were allocated, so it passed for a long time
     without running. It also contradicted its own sibling assertion.
     Rewritten to test what it means.
