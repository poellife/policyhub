Scenarios on a deal, and which one the investors are shown

WHAT THIS ADDS
A Scenarios card on every opportunity. The deal as it stands is the
first row and is never a scenario — it is what the record says, and
every variant is read against it. Add as many as you like, each one
naming a life expectancy, a price, or both:

  The deal as it stands        72 mo   $300,000   64.12%
  If he lives to 96 months     96 mo   $300,000   40.30%
  If we get him to 260         72 mo   $260,000   72.68%
  Both, the pessimistic case   96 mo   $260,000   45.35%

A scenario holds only what it changes. Everything else — the premium
schedule, the dates, the benefit, the second life — is read from the
deal, so correcting the deal corrects every row at once. A figure a
scenario borrowed is set in grey against one it changed, so the eye
lands on what is actually different. Each row is solved by the same
engine the deal itself goes through, so the comparison is like for like.

WHICH ONE THE INVESTORS SEE
The Shown column. Pick a row and that case is what an investor is given
— their deal page, the list behind it, and the one-pager, including the
copy you download to send out. They are told which case it is ("These
figures are the If we get him to 260 case") and are never shown the
rest of the set. Pick the top row to put them back on the deal's own
figures.

Nothing a scenario does touches the deal. The record goes on saying
exactly what it says, which is the point: the afternoon spent trying
numbers out no longer leaves the file claiming something nobody
believes.

WHO CAN DO WHAT
Trying numbers out is the ordinary work of pricing a deal, so anybody
who may edit the deal may add, change and delete scenarios. Deciding
which one an investor is shown changes what the house is offering, so
that is an administrator's alone. An investor can do neither, and sees
no scenario card at all. Choosing one warns you first, in figures, and
says plainly that anybody already shown the deal is not told it moved.
