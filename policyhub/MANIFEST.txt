THE MATURITY SHEET AND THE COMMISSION

  A COMMISSION PER MATURITY, for administrators. On each row of the
  Maturities register, the Commission button takes a percentage of the
  PROFIT AFTER COSTS — what came back, less the purchase price and every
  premium — and charges nothing on a case that lost money. The dialog
  works the percentage out in dollars as you type it.

    Two columns on the register: what it comes to on that case, and the
    NET RETURN TO THE INVESTORS beside the gross one, simple and
    compounded. The net rate is solved again from the cash flows with
    the deduction taken off the final payment on its own date — not the
    gross rate with a percentage taken off it, which is a different and
    wrong number.

    Empty means the owner entity's rate stands. 0 means nothing is
    charged on that case. A manager sees neither column nor button, and
    an investor's register carries no trace of it.

  A ONE-PAGE SUMMARY. The "Sheet" link on each row prints the case and
  its dates, what it cost itemised, what came back, the profit, the
  multiple, both rates, and the cash as it moved.

    SHOW THE COMMISSION ON IT, OR NOT — a tick box on the commission
    dialog, off by default. Left off, the sheet gives the profit and the
    return the investors actually get and says nothing about how it got
    there, so the page can be handed over as it stands. Ticked, it shows
    the gross profit, names the commission, and quotes the return before
    and after it. The register says which of the two a case is set to.

    The sheet is not offered to investors in the portal: their register
    and their statements are their copy, already net.

  Fixed: the note under the rates ran off the right-hand edge of the
  page and lost its last few words. Everything written in sentences on
  that sheet is wrapped to the page now.
