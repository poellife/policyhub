PolicyHub — build 2026-10-07a
Editing the email wording yourself, and who sent us the deal

1. WHAT THE MESSAGES SAY — Settings → The post → "What the messages say"
Every automated email the portal sends, listed on the left; click one
and its wording opens on the right.

  · The subject and body are in boxes you can edit. They start out as
    the exact wording being sent today — taken from the real template,
    not retyped — with the variable parts shown as {{fields}}.
  · The fields that message has are listed as chips under the box.
    Click one to drop it in where the cursor is.
  · Beside it, "As it would arrive" shows the message filled in with
    made-up names and figures, and it changes as you type.
  · "Send me a specimen" posts it to your own address, before or after
    saving, marked [Specimen] so it can never be mistaken for a real one.
  · Save, and the next message of that kind goes out in your words. No
    deployment. "Revert to the default" puts the application's wording
    back; "Put the default back in the box" lets you start over from it
    without saving.

One rule beyond plain substitution: a line whose field has no value for
a particular message is left out of that message entirely. Write "They
said: {{note}}" on its own line and it simply disappears from messages
that carry no note.

What it cannot do, said plainly: branch. The default says "one policy"
or "three policies" where it needs to; your wording says what you type.
And no message has a password, tax number or insured's name among its
fields, so none can be added.

Administrators only — managers and investors are refused, both the
screen and the routes behind it. Every save, revert and specimen is in
the audit log.

2. WHO SENT US THE DEAL — on every opportunity, administrators only
Three boxes in the deal's edit form under "Where it came from": who sent
it (the broker or firm), who there (a person and a number), and when it
arrived. It is the first line on the Deal notes card. Same gate as the
deal notes: a manager is not sent it, cannot see the boxes, and cannot
write to it; an investor never sees it.
