WHAT THIS ADDS

  A MED REVIEW TAB. Every case ever sent to a reviewing doctor, in one
  list, split into what is still out and what has come back. Each row
  names the insured and the case, the reviewer, the date it went, his
  estimate and his call. Opening one shows the whole review: what the
  office sent, what came back, and the button that puts his estimate
  on the case.

  FILES FOR THE DOCTOR. On a review, "Add a file" uploads the records
  themselves — PDF, Word, images or a zip, up to 60 MB each. They appear
  on his screen under "Files sent with this case" and he downloads them
  from there.

    They are filed against the REVIEW, not against the case: they do not
    appear on the Documents tab beside the K-1s, no investor can reach
    them, and the doctor can only open the ones on a review addressed to
    him. Taking one back off is one click.

  WITHDRAWING A REQUEST NOW REMOVES IT FROM HIS SCREEN ENTIRELY — not
  greyed out with a badge, gone, with the records that went with it
  unreachable from that account. The office keeps its own record of
  having sent it.

  THE DOCTOR'S SCREEN IS DOWN TO THREE THINGS: a life expectancy in
  months, a box for his reasoning, and his call at the bottom (Proceed,
  Pass, More records needed). One button, and it says "Review complete".

    Gone from it: "What you have been asked", which said the same thing
    on every case; the median-or-mean question; "How sure are you?"; the
    two impairment lists; "Save a draft" and "I am not reviewing this";
    and the footnote explaining why he sees no price. The rule that
    footnote described has not changed — that account is still sent no
    price, no death benefit and no rate of return.

    Only the estimate is insisted on. A review written on the old longer
    form keeps everything it recorded; the short form does not erase it.

NOTHING TO RUN. Copy the files in and redeploy. The new column applies
itself on boot.
